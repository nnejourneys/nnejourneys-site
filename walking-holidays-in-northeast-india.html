Walking Holidays in Northeast India

From Meghalaya village trails to Arunachal Pradesh forest walks and treks

Overview

Northeast India offers several different kinds of walking journeys

Walking holidays in Northeast India are not one single format. Meghalaya is often the easiest place to start, with village trails, root bridges, waterfalls and day walks that feel active without becoming a major expedition. Arunachal Pradesh adds a more remote feel, with forest walks, frontier valleys and longer treks that can become much more demanding.

The most useful way to choose is by asking what kind of walking experience you want: softer village-based walking, multi-day hill routes, rainforest trekking or remote far-east trails. The site already has examples of each, from Walkers Tour of Meghalaya to Walking Holiday in Eastern Arunachal Pradesh, Namdapha Rainforest Trek and Aeyo Valley Trek.

That range is what makes the region so strong for walkers: you can keep it cultural and accessible, or move toward deeper remoteness and a more demanding trail experience.

What the route feels like

The main walking styles across the region

Meghalaya hill walks

These are some of the most accessible walking holidays in Northeast India: village paths, ridgelines, waterfalls, root bridges and forest descents that work well for travellers who want active days without committing to a full trekking expedition.

Eastern Arunachal forest walks

Eastern Arunachal introduces a quieter, more remote walking style, with village approaches, forest tracks, river valleys and the frontier feel that comes with travelling far beyond standard tourist circuits.

Rainforest trekking

Routes such as Namdapha shift the experience from hill-country walking to full rainforest trekking, where terrain, weather, remoteness and logistics matter much more.

Longer remote treks

Journeys like Aeyo Valley Trek sit at the more demanding end of the range, where trail days become longer, access is harder and the walking experience is shaped by true remoteness rather than simple scenic day routes.

Practical fit

How to choose the right walking route

If you want the softest entry point, start with Meghalaya. It is often the best option for travellers who enjoy walking, nature and culture, but do not want a highly technical trekking challenge.

If you want stronger remoteness, look at the Arunachal walking routes and rainforest treks. These tend to suit travellers who are happy with simpler logistics, less infrastructure and a journey that feels much further from the usual circuit.

For the best season, use our guide to the best time to visit Northeast India. In broad terms, October to March is the safest recommendation for most walking and trekking routes.
