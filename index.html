The region

The region at a glance

Seven states, extraordinary cultural range, Himalayas, foothills, rainforests, rivers, tea country and some of India's least explored places.

The Region

This little known corner of the Indian Subcontinent shares international borders with Bhutan, Tibet, China, Myanmar and Bangladesh, and is connected to mainland India by a small sliver of land in West Bengal popularly known as the Chicken's Neck.

Consisting of eight states, the region is formed like a gigantic basin with the Brahmaputra and Barak valleys taking center stage. The northern border with Tibet and China is barricaded by the giant Eastern Himalayan landmass. In the east, the densely forested Patkai Bum and Naga Patkai ranges bordering Burma run south toward the Chaifil Tlang and Uiphum Tlang ranges in Mizoram, which continue east toward the Chin Hills and Arakan Yoma of Burma.

In the south, the Jaintia, Khasi and Garo Hills of Meghalaya border the rich plains of Bangladesh and complete the formation. This region is incidentally the closest that the Himalayan mountain range gets to the coast. This geographical factor, combined with some of the highest rainfall on the planet, has resulted in one of the greatest diversities of flora and fauna and, in turn, a diverse mix of people.

The People

Perhaps the same reason that makes this region so rich in its natural diversity also makes it the most ethnic, linguistic and culturally diverse region in the Indian sub-continent.

For example, Arunachal Pradesh itself has over 26 major tribes with over 100 sub-tribes and clans speaking some 50 dialects. These tribes provide a cultural bridge between India and South East Asia, China, Inner Asia and Burma through ethnic and linguistic connections, as many migrated here from the north and east over the centuries.

These eight states together constitute a single linguistic region with about 220 languages across three language groups: Indo-Aryan, Sino-Tibetan and Austric. The earliest recorded history can be traced back only to the Ahom kings of the 16th century, who ruled the Brahmaputra Valley. Unlike the rest of India, the majority here bear Mongoloid features.

The Environment

This region has a predominantly humid sub-tropical climate with hot, humid summers, severe monsoons and fairly mild winters. Along with the west coast of India, it has some of the Indian sub-continent's last remaining rainforests, supporting diverse flora and fauna and several endemic species.

It has been pooled under the Eastern Himalayan and the Indo-Burmese biodiversity hotspots. The close proximity of the High Himalayas to the coast has created a compact stretch of land with a multitude of vegetation types, from alpine tundra in the northern regions to tropical and sub-tropical rainforests in the lower areas.

The central river valleys support grasslands and large wetland areas. This variety of ecosystems has created a haven for biodiversity, and the region also boasts one of the highest densities of protected wildlife reserves in the country. Kaziranga, Manas, Nameri and Namdapha National Parks are some of the better known ones.

It also has some of the highest densities of tigers, elephants and the great one-horned rhinoceros, the only ape species found in India, and wildlife parks known for hosting five feline species.
